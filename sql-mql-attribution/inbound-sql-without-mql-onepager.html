The core finding

This is a definition and data-capture problem, not a tracking-pixel problem.

Riverside conflates two different MQLs. The funnel-stage MQL is simply an inbound Pre-Op, so by that definition every one of these SQLs already is an MQL. The marketing-attribution MQL is a separate form record carrying first-visit UTM and channel, linked by identity, and that is what is missing. When we classify the 37% of records that carry an AE source note, only about 5% are genuine marketing inbound that failed to join. The rest never had a form-MQL by nature.

The win is not recovered ad credit (a modest $5.1K/mo). It is de-polluting the inbound number by removing roughly 109 non-marketing records that today inflate Inbound MQL counts and distort MQL-to-SQL conversion and CAC, plus making every completed-meeting SQL visible in the funnel with an honest basis.

Recommended framework

  • Tiered waterfall assigns an auditable basis to all 652: identity stitch, CRM reclassification, text classification, blank-residual re-join, unrecoverable flag.
  • Redefine reporting MQL as acquisition type Marketing Inbound, not Last Touch Source Inbound.
  • Quarantine Paid behind a deterministic ad-click id. No free-text or model may ever write Paid.
  • Debit vs relabel: debit outbound and CS out of inbound, relabel PLG but keep it in the funnel. Never drop a completed-meeting SQL.
  • Forward capture beats backfill: required source enum plus auto-detection of PLG, outbound, and CS at Pre-Op creation.

One thing to act on now

  • Outbound is leaking into Inbound, and it is new. 32 of the 36 mis-sourced records were created in 2026 (2025: 1, 2024: 3). This is a recent BD-motion or tagging change.
  • The funnel shows only 283 outbound SQLs in four years against 14,934 inbound, which is implausibly low and confirms the under-tagging.
  • Fix it forward in the source logic, do not subtract it uniformly across history, and dual-track the time series at cutover.
