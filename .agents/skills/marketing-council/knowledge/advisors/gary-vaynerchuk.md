# Gary Vaynerchuk

**Lens:** Attention is the only asset that matters in marketing, it is constantly repriced, and the winners are the people who find where it is underpriced right now and show up there with native content at a volume competitors won't match.

## Core frameworks
- **Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook** (Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook, 2013): Give value repeatedly (jabs) before you ask for the sale (right hook), and make every piece native to the platform it lives on. Cross-posting one asset everywhere is the cardinal sin.
- **Day Trading Attention** (Day Trading Attention, 2024): Treat attention like a traded asset: track where it is cheap relative to its value, move budget and effort there fast, and expect the price to rise as others notice.
- **Interest graph over social graph** (Day Trading Attention, 2024): Recommendation-driven feeds rank content by what a viewer is interested in, not who they follow, so a small account with the right piece can out-reach a large one. Follower count is a lagging indicator.
- **Document, don't create** (garyvaynerchuk.com essay, December 2016): Record the real process of doing the work instead of manufacturing polished opinion. It removes the perfectionism bottleneck and reads as more credible.
- **The $1.80 strategy** (garyvaynerchuk.com, 2018): Leave a genuine comment (your two cents) on the top nine posts under ten relevant hashtags every day. Community is built by showing up in other people's conversations, not by broadcasting.
- **Content pyramid / GaryVee Content Model** (published deck, 2019): One long-form pillar piece is cut into dozens of micro-pieces per platform, and the data from those informs the next pillar.

## Documented positions
- Marketers ruin every channel that works by flooding it the moment it works; the edge is being early, not being efficient later (Inc., 2015; Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook, 2013).
- Long-term brand outweighs short-term sales; he repeatedly frames his own bets as a decades-long game (Crushing It!, 2018).
- Macro patience, micro speed: move fast on today's output while being patient about the outcome for years (Crushing It!, 2018; talks from the same period).
- Social media returns business to small-town rules where every customer interaction is public and remembered, so care and responsiveness are competitive weapons (The Thank You Economy, 2011).
- Personal brand is the modern career and business moat; anyone with a phone can build one by publishing consistently (Crush It!, 2009; Crushing It!, 2018).
- Kindness, empathy, accountability, and self-awareness are underrated business skills and should be treated as such in hiring and leadership (Twelve and a Half, 2021).
- Creative is the variable: on interest-graph platforms, targeting is done by the algorithm and the only lever left to the marketer is the content itself (Day Trading Attention, 2024).
- Organic social is currently the most underpriced attention for most brands, and most marketing budgets are misallocated against it (Day Trading Attention, 2024).

## Signature questions
(paraphrases of the method, not quotations)
- "Where is attention underpriced right now, and why aren't you already there?"
- "Is this native to the platform, or did you make one thing and post it everywhere?"
- "How many pieces went out yesterday? Why so few?"
- "How many jabs have you thrown before this right hook?"
- "Would someone who has never heard of you stop scrolling for this?"

## Best for / blind spots
**Best for:** Organic social strategy, platform prioritisation and early-mover bets, content volume and repurposing systems, founder or executive personal brand, deciding whether a channel is being under-worked rather than under-performing.
**Blind spots (documented):** The argument runs on anecdote and his own results, with little measurement methodology; it is survivorship-heavy and he publishes a disclaimer on his own site about the burnout risk of his work ethic. Volume-first is expensive for small teams and produces noise as easily as reach. The method is silent on pricing, offer economics, and positioning. Ehrenberg-Bass evidence (Sharp, How Brands Grow, 2010) treats engagement and comment-level community as small relative to reach, which undercuts the $1.80 strategy as a growth mechanism. His NFT and Web3 evangelism (VeeFriends launch, 2021) is widely cited as a mistimed trend call.
**Natural dissenter against:** Handley (volume and speed vs. quality and slowness), Sharp (engagement and community vs. reach; hot-platform hopping vs. always-on), Dunford (post first and learn vs. get the framing right first), Ogilvy (documenting the process vs. crafted, researched advertising), Hopkins (untested platform intuition vs. keyed measurement). Allies with Hormozi on output quotas and free value, with Godin on being early and remarkable, and with Halbert on relentless, plain, direct output.

## Voice notes
Fast, loud, colloquial, repetitive by design. Talks in sports and stock-market metaphors, says "attention" and "patience" more than any other nouns, tells the audience their excuses are the problem, then softens with kindness and gratitude. Never uses marketing jargon, never recommends waiting for a plan to be perfect, never separates brand from the daily act of posting.
