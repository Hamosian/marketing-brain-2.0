# Register: leadership narrative

Writing for the CEO, the exec team, the board or investors. A board slide, a QBR narrative, a funding case, an escalation. The reader is short on time, numerate, and will be making a decision.

**Lead with the decision you want, not the analysis that produced it.** The body earns the ask; it does not build up to it. If the reader stops after paragraph one, they should already know what you are asking for.

## Shape

- **The ask, then the number, then the reasoning.** In that order, always.
- **Own the bad news first.** A narrative that leads with wins and buries the miss reads as spin and costs the credibility of every number after it. Name the miss, size it, say what you are doing.
- **One number per claim, sourced.** Every figure carries its source and as-of date per `references/evidence-standards.md`. A board figure that cannot be traced is worse than no figure.
- **Name the trade-off you accepted.** Leadership is choosing between costs. A narrative with no cost reads as incomplete.
- **Say what you need from them.** Budget, a decision, air cover, a hire. Never leave the ask implied.

## Altitude

- **Write about the operation, not the tactics.** Not "we changed the anchor on the choice email" but "self-serve leads convert better when we stop pushing them up a tier".
- **No internal shorthand.** Register-level jargon, skill names, tool names and internal codenames do not travel. Say the thing.
- **Segment and motion, not channel minutiae.** They care that PLG activation moved, not which subject line won.

## What kills it

- **Hedging.** "It's possible that", "we may see", "directionally". State the number and its confidence, or do not state it.
- **A wall of context before the point.** They have less time than you think.
- **Unlabelled optimism.** A forecast is a forecast; label it as one.
- **Metric definitions invented on the spot.** The analytics team owns the canonical ones. Route through `/rivermind:ask` before a definition reaches a board deck.
