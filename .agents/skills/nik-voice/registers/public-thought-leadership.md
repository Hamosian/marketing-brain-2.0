# Register: public thought leadership

LinkedIn posts, newsletter sections, bylines, conference abstracts. The reader is a peer who owes Nir nothing and will stop reading at the first generic sentence.

**The bar: say something a sharp peer does not already know.** If a reader would think "no kidding" or "everyone says this", the piece has no reason to exist. This register is the one where non-obviousness is the whole game.

## Shape

- **Hook in line 1.** It has to work standalone, before the "see more" cut. No preamble, no context-setting.
- **White space aggressively.** One idea per line, short paragraphs. Long blocks do not get read.
- **Close on a forward hook or a real question.** "That's the next piece, I'll share it once I've pressure-tested it." Or "Have you ever mapped your do-nothing competitor? Genuinely curious what you found." Never "Hope this was helpful, let me know your thoughts."
- **Max one or two bold phrases.** Bolding everything bolds nothing.
- **No emoji** unless the context is clearly playful.

## The moves that carry this register

- **Parallel negatives to kill an assumption.** "Two words. Not a new page. Not a new flow. Not a redesign. Two words." Use it to destroy the expected answer before giving the real one. Use it once per piece; twice is a drumbeat.
- **Sounds impossible, then true.** Make the bold specific claim, then immediately validate it with the real example. Do not hedge up front. "You could generate 500 qualified sales meetings from a single event. That actually happened. Here's the setup."
- **Editorial voice inside lists.** Never a bare list. Each item carries a short reaction: "**Negative ICP defined:** who you explicitly don't serve. Most people skip this. It's the reason your pipeline fills with deals that never close."
- **Credibility first on a framework post.** "These are my three favourite X as someone who has Y" beats leading with the opinion.
- **Honesty about failure.** "I tried everything. New copy. New design. New flows. The conversion rate barely budged." Being wrong in public is the cheapest credibility there is, and AI never volunteers it.

## What kills it

- **Vague numbers.** "Significant results" and "3x pipeline in 6 months" are the same sentence with different credibility. Always the second one.
- **Rhythm that never breaks.** Perfectly alternating short and long is its own tell. Break the pattern somewhere.
- **Explaining the term.** "As you may know, ICP stands for..." Trust the reader.
- **Naming a client or number you cannot publish.** Check before it ships.
