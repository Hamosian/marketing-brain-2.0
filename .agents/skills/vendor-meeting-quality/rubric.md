# Vendor brief rubric, v1.2 (2026-09-23, after the 14-22 September calls)

**The headline output is the bad-brief flag defined in `SKILL.md`, whose rules come from `knowledge/ziff-brief-patterns-2026-09.md`. The score below is supporting detail, kept so future batches can test whether the score adds anything the flag misses.**

Scores what the vendor knew and wrote down before the meeting. 100 points, five components, hard flags force Red. Weights come from Nir's read of 31 Ziff calls (1 July to 14 September 2026: 5 qualified, 15 weak, 11 unqualified; 22 with no fitting recording programme, 23 with no budget this financial year) and from the outcome backfill in `knowledge/baseline-2026-09.md`. Weights change only through `calibrate`, logged in `data/calibration-log.md`.

**Verdict:** Green 70 and above. Amber 45 to 69. Red under 45, or any hard flag.

## 1. Programme fit, 35 points

The component that prevents the most bad meetings. "Do you do video?" gets a yes from a company shooting phone clips at a trade show, so score the format and the cadence, not the word video.

| Evidence in the brief | Points |
|---|---|
| One of podcast, webinar, interview or customer testimonial, running at least monthly, **with evidence: how often, the tool it runs on, or a recent example** | 35 |
| A programme described with no cadence, no tool and no example ("produces video content", "a dedicated team producing webinars") | 20 |
| A recurring video programme of another kind (how-to, training, creator or influencer content) produced by two or more people | 28 |
| A dated series starting within 90 days, with a named host | 25 |
| "Planning within 90 days", no date and no host | 12 |
| "Interested", "exploring", "wants to learn how to set up", no date, **or a programme that is paused or described as "future" / "potential future"** | 8 |
| Written content only, or no programme at all | 0 |

The July data behind the low rows: five of seven "planning" calls had no host and no date, and none converted.

## 2. Incumbent and relevance, 20 points

| Evidence | Points |
|---|---|
| Nothing, Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, phone, Clipchamp, Loom, Canva, a freelancer, GoToWebinar with no contract mentioned, **and a programme exists (fit 25 or more)** | 20 |
| Nothing in place and no programme either (fit 12 or under): "nothing to replace" is not a buying signal | 10 |
| A platform on month-to-month, or a contract ending within six months | 15 |
| Not stated | 7 |
| A platform under contract, end date not stated | 5 |
| A platform locked in for more than six months and they are happy with it (Livestorm at Fiery, Contrast at IntegrityNext) | 0 |

"Have you looked at Riverside before" earns no points. When the answer is yes, write it into `flags` as `prior Riverside contact` so the AE is told. Four of the 31 had used or priced Riverside and none of it reached the AE.

## 3. Owner and team, 25 points

Owner, 15: the prospect decides on video tools, or the owner is named and joining, 15. **A manager-level title with no stated decision authority scores 3, not a default:** in the 14-22 September calls every such prospect ended with "I need to take this to my director or manager". The prospect recommends and names who decides, or is forwarding the invite to the owner, 8. "Ownership may sit elsewhere", 3. Not the owner and cannot name one, 0.

Team, 10: two or more people producing in-house, or an agency they want to replace, 10. Not stated, 3. One person doing it as a side project, 0.

## 4. Budget, 10 points

Budget for a video or webinar platform this financial year, or spend over five thousand a year on video today, 10. Not stated, 3. Next financial year already closed, pre-revenue, "need to build a business case", 0.

Ziff agreed to push for budget, not to gate on it, so this stays a small component. It is still scored because it is the second biggest reason a held meeting went nowhere.

## 5. Persona and size, 10 points

| Evidence | Points |
|---|---|
| Head or director of marketing, content, brand, communications, video, production, multimedia or creative services, or the manager who personally runs the programme, at 51 employees or more | 10 |
| A generic marketing manager at 201 employees or more | 8 |
| A generic marketing manager at 51 to 200 | 7 |
| Under 50 employees, any title | 3 |
| A role that wasted a meeting before: regional field marketer who is not the tool owner, agency or event producer whose clients pick the platform, employer-engagement lead, tech-department employee, project manager at a label | 0 |

The backfill says size and level barely separate winners from losers on their own (managers at 51 to 200 produced most of the wins and most of the losses), which is why this component is worth 10 and programme fit is worth 35.

## Hard flags, any one forces Red

- **Off-fit problem, and no fitting programme.** The pain is joining a meeting, connectivity, mobile agents on bad connections, animations or visualisations, AI generating a podcast from text, and there is no podcast, webinar, interview or recurring video programme underneath it (fit under 25). Riverside records; it does not fix attendance. When a real programme exists and the off-fit ask sits beside it, it is an AE note, not a Red: Nodor scored 93 on the programme and the AE found a live opportunity behind an AI-product-video wish list.
- **Self-serve profile.** One seat, one person recording and editing alone, an expectation of under a hundred a month, "we were paying thirty or forty pounds before".
- **Agency, producer or reseller whose clients choose the platform**, unless the brief states they want to standardise their own client work on one platform and names the volume.
- **Prior open opportunity** on the contact or company (from the Step 3.5 verdict), or a contact who has left.
- **Brief missing.** The body carries no discovery answers at all, or only an attachment. Score what is there, flag it, and the feedback asks for the body.

`brief incomplete` (one or two of the three answers missing) is a flag that does not force Red. It is still the first line of feedback.

## Calibration batch 1 (47 briefs, 5 August to 17 September 2026, scored 19 September)

Scored from the LHO PDFs after Nir downloaded them. 22 of the 47 meetings had a next-day label from the Gong brief and the Pre-Op stage.

| Verdict (v1) | qualified | weak | unqualified | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green (5 labelled) | 2 | 3 | 0 | No Green produced a bad meeting. Greens are safe to take. |
| Amber (11 labelled) | 1 | 4 | 6 | The bad meetings hide in Amber. Every unqualified Amber scored 53 to 67, and 19 of those points were defaults on questions the brief never answered. |
| Red (6 labelled) | 2 | 2 | 2 | Two misses: Nodor (off-fit hard flag on a company with a real programme) and FiveTran (not-the-owner flag when she was forwarding the invite to the owner). Both were rule wording, not weights. |

Brief compliance: 8 of 47 briefs answered all three agreed questions. Owner and team size were the two answers most often missing. Five briefs omitted employee size. One brief carried another company's prospect name, and two carried two different meeting times in the same body.

Changes made for v1.1, all from this batch: silence defaults reduced (incumbent 7, team 3, budget 3), "nothing in place" only worth 20 when a programme exists, the off-fit hard flag narrowed to cases with no fitting programme, and "forwarding to the owner" scores 8. Thresholds unchanged. The rows in the ledger keep their v1 scores; new briefs score on v1.1.

## Calibration batch 2 (calls held 14-22 September 2026)

Nine held meetings: 1 qualified, 4 weak, 4 unqualified. Scored against the briefs, v1.1 flagged 2 of the 3 scoreable unqualified meetings and over-scored two Greens. What the calls taught, and what v1.2 changed:

- **Briefs overstate activity.** Three briefs described a running programme the call did not find: a "dedicated social media team producing webinars" was one person on an iPhone, "M&A commentary and client testimonials" was a paused podcast and "white space", "producing how-to videos" was "a lack of video marketing". A programme claim without cadence, tool or example now scores 20, not 28 to 35.
- **Future money.** Three meetings were next year's budget (May 2027, next fiscal year, future planning). None of the briefs asked. New soft flag `budget next year` when the brief says it; the missing budget answer is feedback.
- **Managers who are not owners.** Obsidian, Abercrombie and Ocado all ended with "I'll take it to my director". Manager-level with no stated authority now scores owner 3 and raises the soft flag `owner not on the call`.
- **Nonprofits** with no tools budget went to self-serve: added to the self-serve flag.

Re-scored under v1.2, all three scoreable unqualified meetings carry a flag, the qualified one carries none, and three of four weak meetings carry a soft flag naming the reason they were weak. The one still missed is a brief that overstated a Marketing Director's programme; nothing in the brief could show it, which is feedback for the vendor, not a rule.

## What the score does not do

It does not predict a win. Ten of 67 held meetings promoted and five won across seven months, so the win signal is too thin to calibrate against until well into 2027. The score predicts the next-day label. Day-30 and final outcomes are tracked to keep the rubric honest and to build the renewal case.
