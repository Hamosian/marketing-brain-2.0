# Business-edge hooks

Pre-cleared, benefit-led "why Business" lines for the drafting step, organised the way a buyer thinks: four motivations, matched to role. Source material is the internal Pro-vs-Business enablement deck (Aug 2026); every line here has been run through `demo-reply-fact-check` and traced to the verified-claims ledger. **This file does not replace the Business-edge rules in `SKILL.md` Step 4.6 / PART 3 - it feeds them.** The shape is still one anchored edge with a short benefit clause, then two more named as a bare list.

## The umbrella framing (per Nir)

The four pillars are not four unrelated feature sets. They are the four ways one sentence is true:

> "Our Business product is the stronger, more professional, more collaborative, and more secure version of our platform for those who are really implementing a serious and robust content strategy versus doing more of a hobbyist podcast or livestream series."

**stronger / more professional** → pillar 2, production control. **more collaborative** → pillar 1, team collaboration. **more secure** → pillar 3, enterprise security. Pillar 4, white-glove service, is what carries a serious rollout. Business is the same platform at a higher grade, never a different product.

Use this to set the altitude of the copy and to choose the pillar. Do not paste the sentence at a lead, never describe the lead (the word "hobbyist" is internal only), and do not use the seriousness axis when routing someone down to self-serve. Full rules in `SKILL.md` PART 3 → Product facts.

## How to use

1. Match the lead's role to a pillar below (form_comment first, job title second).
2. Take that pillar's **anchor line** as the draft's middle sentence. It states the outcome, never the feature's parts. **Paste it WHOLE.** A cleared anchor's qualifying clause is part of the claim, not decoration: truncating pillar 1 to "one shared workspace" drops the roles clause that does the tier work and turns a verified claim into one the lead can disprove from their own settings page. Fit the anchor into nik-voice by changing the sentence around it, never by paraphrasing the anchor. Canonical rule: `SKILL.md` PART 3 -> "Paste the anchor WHOLE".
3. Name **two trailing edges** from the same pillar as a bare list, no explanation.
4. Never stack pillars. One anchor, two names, done.

The anchor is a starting point, not a script. If the form_comment names a specific pain, answer that pain and pull the anchor from whichever pillar fits it - not from the role default.

**"Never stack pillars" governs the email, not the pillar the anchor came from.** It bans naming three edges from three pillars, which reads as a feature dump. It does NOT require the anchor and the trailing two to share a pillar. When the form_comment pulls the anchor away from the role's default pillar, the trailing two may still come from that default: the lead's own words set the anchor, the role sets the neighbourhood. A marketing title whose comment asks for professional webinars and podcasts legitimately anchors on the producer role (pillar 2) and trails with team collaboration and async recording (pillar 1). The fact-check gate reads step 3 strictly and will flag the crossing; that is a note for Nir, not a block, so long as all three edges are on the verified list and the anchor answers what the lead wrote.

## The four pillars

### 1. Team collaboration - Marketing, Content, Ops, agencies, anyone producing with a team
- **Anchor:** your team works in one shared workspace, with roles controlling who can access which production.
- **Trailing edges:** async recording · the producer role.
- **Never split "one shared workspace" from the roles clause.** The workspace half on its own is the CONTRADICTED shape (ledger 2026-08-14). Editor add-on seats are buyable in-product on Pro, Grow and Webinar, and the self-serve ceiling is one seat plus two Editor add-ons, so multi-person record-and-edit is not the gate and below four people self-serve is the honest answer. The roles clause does the tier work, because role-based team management across productions is gated at every seat count. The full line ships unchanged at any plan state, free included.
- **The billing contrast is retired: do not restore it (per Nir, 2026-09-02).** "Everyone records and edits in one shared account instead of each person paying for their own" was this pillar's anchor and it is banned twice over. SKILL.md Step 4.6's billing-reframe rule forbids framing Business as a cheaper way to buy seats, because it walks the lead to "then just sell me another seat" and "need does not justify enterprise cost" is 47% of all losses. It also asserts a billing state HubSpot cannot confirm. Pointed at a finance title it is a guided tour to the objection.
- **"team roles" is no longer a trailing edge here.** The corrected anchor already names roles, so listing it again double-counts one capability, the same padding failure pillar 2 bans for the producer camera/mic case.
- Why it wins: "team collaboration" is a verified Business-only pillar, expressed as access control rather than seat economics.
- **This pillar is the DEFAULT for a lead on a paid self-serve plan, and pillar 4 is not (per Nir, 2026-09-02).** An existing Pro, Grow or Webinar customer asking for a Business demo is almost always asking how more than one person gets in, or how the production stops living on one person. Anchor on collaboration and reach for pillar 4 (CSM, priority support, book-a-producer) only when the lead's own words are about wanting hands-on help. The failure this fixes: a solo Pro podcast host who wrote "ease and simplicity of self-production" got a book-a-producer anchor when collaboration was the answer in front of us. That anchor is now the pillar's anchor for every plan state, so a paid self-serve lead gets it by default; the retired shared-account contrast is the one such a lead disproves fastest, from their own settings page (editor add-on seats).

### 2. Production control - Producers, show designers, exec producers, live-event teams
- **Anchor:** a producer can run the whole session from backstage, managing every guest without appearing on camera.
- **Trailing edges:** custom frame rates · XML/AAF timeline export.
- **Adjusting a guest's camera and mic mid-session is PART OF the producer role, not a separate edge (Ann Tsunakawa, 2026-08-31, source `the-producer-role-details`).** So it belongs inside the anchor clause, never as a third bullet beside it: naming both double-counts one capability and reads like padding. When the lead's pain is "we can see the guest's audio is wrong and can't fix it", widen the anchor to say the producer fixes it from backstage, and keep the trailing two as they are.
- Why it wins: the producer role is the single clearest Business gate for a production title, and it maps to real backstage control.

### 3. Enterprise security - IT, CTO, CISO, Security, Procurement
- **Anchor:** your team signs in through SSO with SCIM provisioning, so IT manages access centrally as people join and leave.
- **Trailing edges:** SOC 2 report access under NDA · a contractual security review.
- Why it wins: SSO/SCIM is a real Business gate, and the report-access-under-NDA + security-review process is what an IT buyer actually needs to clear procurement.
- **Read the security guardrail below before writing anything for this pillar.** The certifications themselves are not a plan gate.

### 4. White-glove service - first-time enterprise buyers, live-event owners, "who supports us" asks
- **Anchor:** you get a dedicated CSM and hands-on onboarding, plus a priority support queue built for live shows and critical recordings.
- **Trailing edges:** unlimited studios · book a producer on demand.
- **"Unlimited studios" is RESTORED and is the correct wording (Ann Tsunakawa, 2026-08-31).** This row previously said "unlimited shows and hosting", which `SKILL.md` had retired as unsourced, and the two files disagreed while both were live. Ann settled it: no plan caps shows, every self-serve plan caps **studios**, and Business gives unlimited studios, which is what makes shows and hosting unlimited downstream. Pitch the studio. Never pitch "unlimited hosting" on its own to a Pro, Grow or Webinar customer, who already has hosting.
- Why it wins: 24/7 support covers every tier, so the Business-specific part is **priority** and the named human (CSM + onboarding), not "24/7". Never imply self-serve users have no support.

## Reading the Business vs Pro one-pager (Ann, 2026-08, `[Sales & B2B] Business vs Pro _2026_08_One Pager.pdf`)

**It compares Business against Pro ONLY, so a row Business wins does NOT mean Business-only.** This is the single most dangerous thing about the document and the reason two of our files disagreed for weeks. Anything sitting on Grow or Webinar beats Pro and lands in the Business column looking like a gate. Confirmed cases: **HubSpot integration** is Webinar and up, **multistreaming** starts at Grow. Both appear under Business on the one-pager and neither is a Business edge.

Use the one-pager for the *shape* of the Business story and for Ann's wording. Never promote a row from it to the verified Business-only list in `SKILL.md` without a tier confirmation from Ann or the live pricing page. **Slides upload into the studio is now settled as Business-only (Nir, 2026-09-20)** and has been removed from the list below; it is a legitimate Business edge and sits on the verified list in `SKILL.md`. Rows still unconfirmed above Pro, so **do not pitch them as Business-only**: live studio polls, chat moderation, webinar on-demand, full 16:9 frame, faster processing, simultaneous recording sessions, private chat, teleprompter for guests as well as hosts, admin-managed sharing permissions, and password-protect / expire / revoke on share-for-review links.

## Do not ship - the deck overclaims here

The enablement deck is an internal comparison; some of its contrasts are wrong or unverified as plan gates. These fail the fact-check and must not reach a lead:

- **Security certifications as a Pro-vs-Business edge - FALSE.** Riverside is SOC 2 Type 2 compliant (since 2021) and ISO/IEC 27001 certified (since 2022) across the whole platform. A Pro user's data sits on the same certified infrastructure. Never say or imply "Business is SOC 2 / ISO 27001 and Pro isn't." The Business-gated items are **SSO/SCIM**, **SOC 2 report access under NDA**, and the **security review** - pitch those, not the badges.
- **"End-to-end encryption" as a Business edge - UNVERIFIED.** Encrypted connections are platform-wide posture, not a Business feature. Do not use it as a differentiator.
- **Marketo / HubSpot integration - RESOLVED, and they split (Ann Tsunakawa, 2026-08-31).** **Salesforce and Marketo are Business only.** **HubSpot is Webinar and up**, so it is NOT a Business edge and must never be pitched as one. The Business API stays Business-gated. This is the trap the one-pager sets: it lists all three under Business because it compares Business against Pro, and HubSpot beats Pro without being Business-only.
- **Multistreaming as a Business edge - CONTRADICTED.** It starts at **Grow**, so it beats Pro without being Business-only. It appears on the Business-vs-Pro one-pager for exactly that reason. Skip it.
- **Studio branding as a Business edge - CONTRADICTED.** Placing a logo, colour, or overlay is All-plans (ledger). The only narrow Business branding gates are the cross-studio brand default and custom brand fonts. Do not lead with "branding."
- **The commercial/legal slide is internal-only.** The AI-training opt-out addendum, MSA/DPA/NDA, IP indemnity, and insurance terms are marked "FOR INTERNAL USE ONLY" in the deck. None of it goes in a prospect email. (The product toggle to disable AI features is a real Business-line item and may be mentioned for a privacy-led ask; the negotiated legal addendum may not.)
