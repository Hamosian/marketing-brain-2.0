# Drafting style digest - Smart Brevity + Gary Provost rhythm, distilled for one-paragraph emails

The operative checks from `plugin-skills/writing-optimizer/references/smart-brevity.md` and
`plugin-skills/writing-optimizer/references/gary-provost-rhythm.md`, compressed for the inbound-demo-reply drafting pass. This digest is the
default style pass for every draft. Open the full references ONLY when a draft is genuinely
struggling after two passes against this digest - for a 3-5 sentence email, this digest is the check.

## Smart Brevity - cut to the bone

Run these checks in order on every draft:

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence after "Hi [Name]," must carry the single most
   important thing (the answer, the hook, the reason for writing). If the reader could delete
   sentence 1 and lose nothing, the lede is buried - promote the real point.
2. **One idea per sentence.** A sentence holding two ideas gets split or one idea gets cut.
   Read each sentence and name its one idea; if you name two, it fails.
3. **No stacked front-clauses.** A sentence must not open with a subordinate wind-up before the
   subject ("With your team recording weekly, and since you mentioned X, ..."). One opening clause
   maximum, and prefer none: start on the subject and verb. This is the specific failure the full
   reference exists to catch - check it explicitly, sentence by sentence.
4. **Kill hedges and filler.** Cut: "just", "really", "actually", "I think", "sort of", "quick
   question", "I wanted to reach out to", "I hope this finds you well", "as mentioned", and every
   qualifier that softens without informing. State it or drop it.
5. **Strong verbs, no nominalizations.** "We record locally" beats "recording is done locally".
   Prefer a concrete verb over a be-verb + abstraction ("provides the ability to" → "lets you").
6. **The delete test.** For every sentence: if deleting it changes nothing the reader needs or
   does, delete it. Personalization that would still be true with the personalization removed
   is filler (this mirrors the Hard-rules "every sentence must earn its place").
7. **Word budget.** Body of 3-5 sentences, none over ~25 words. If a sentence needs a breath
   mid-read, split it or cut it.

## Gary Provost rhythm - make it read aloud cleanly

1. **Vary sentence length.** Three same-length sentences in a row read like a machine wrote them.
   The fix is contrast: follow a longer sentence with a short one. Short sentences land the punch.
2. **The read-aloud test.** Read the body aloud once. Anywhere you stumble, run out of breath, or
   flatten into monotone is a rewrite point. This one test catches most rhythm failures.
3. **Vary sentence openings.** Two consecutive sentences must not open with the same word or the
   same construction (both starting "You...", both starting "We...", both starting with a
   subordinate clause). Change the second one's entry point.
4. **Active voice by default.** Passive only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
5. **End on strength.** The last content word before the CTA should be a concrete noun or verb,
   not a qualifier. Don't let the paragraph trail off into "and things like that" energy.

## Order of operations

Brevity first, rhythm second: cut everything cuttable, then tune what survives. Never add words
back for rhythm - fix rhythm by reordering and splitting, not by padding. All PART 3 hard rules
(no em/en dashes, contractions, banned phrases, fixed closer) outrank anything here.
