# Format playbooks

Load only the section for the classified format. Each gives the beat vocabulary
for the structure map and the 5-6 structural checks that matter most for that
surface. These are the rubric - score against them, don't improvise new
criteria mid-critique.

## YouTube script

**Beat vocabulary:** hook, promise (title/thumbnail contract), stakes, setup,
tension, mini-payoff, re-hook, escalation, climax, resolution, next-action
pivot.

**Checks:**
1. **The first 30-60 seconds pay the title/thumbnail promise** and front-load
   the stakes. Retention is won or lost there. If the video opens with
   channel/creator preamble before the promise, flag beat 1. For the
   second-by-second anatomy of the opening (interrupt, gap, stake, the four
   silent exits, the 15-45s valley) see `hook-retention-psychology.md`.
2. **The hook makes consequences personal to the viewer** - "here's what this
   means for you," not just drama.
3. **Setup → tension → payoff cycles every 60-90 seconds**, with mini-payoffs
   so the viewer never waits for the end for all release. Top-tier retention
   practice re-hooks every 20-40s with a new question, twist, or escalation.
4. **Escalation, not a flat list.** Each segment raises the stakes over the
   last: bigger number, harder constraint, closer to the promised payoff.
   Peer segments that could be reordered freely = a flat list, flag it.
5. **Loop hygiene:** the title-loop stays open until the climax; segment loops
   close on schedule (see the loop ledger in the method file).
6. **The ending pivots into the next action before energy drops.** A long
   thanks/summary outro is a cut, not a beat.

## Ad script (paid social / video)

**Beat vocabulary:** hook (pattern interrupt), pain/outcome naming, agitation,
turn/reveal, proof, offer, CTA.

**Checks:**
1. **The first 3 seconds carry a named pain or outcome plus a visual pattern
   interrupt.** Healthy hook rate is roughly 25-35%; under ~15% the platform
   punishes the ad. If the concept can't state what the first 3 seconds show
   and say, that's the finding. Hook mechanics and the muted-feed overlay rule:
   `hook-retention-psychology.md`.
2. **One clean structure, fully executed:** Problem-Agitate-Solve or
   Before-After-Bridge. Check the agitation actually *deepens the cost* of the
   problem rather than restating it in new words.
3. **Sound-off test:** does the argument survive as text and visuals alone?
   Most feed video plays muted.
4. **Product and proof appear early**, not as a third-act reveal.
5. **One ad = one claim = one CTA.** A second idea is a second ad, not a
   longer script.
6. **The hook is separable** - it can be swapped and tested independently of
   the body.

## Newsletter

**Beat vocabulary:** subject-line loop, lede/personal stake, context, the
"aha" (core insight), development/proof, application steps, close/action.

**Checks:**
1. **Single-idea rule.** One big idea developed beats three grazed. The
   working structure: relatable-problem hook → insight ("aha") → actionable
   application.
2. **The subject line opens a genuine information gap the body closes.**
   Curiosity with payoff, not bait - a gap the piece never fills is the worst
   outcome for list trust.
3. **The lede earns line two:** a personal stake or surprising claim in
   sentence one, not throat-clearing.
4. **The "aha" is quotable.** Can you point at the one sentence that IS the
   idea? If the critique can't quote it, the newsletter doesn't have one -
   that's the top finding.
5. **Ends with one specific action or reflection**, not a summary of what was
   just said.
6. **Sponsored-newsletter extra:** the sponsor must enter as a tool inside the
   writer's own story (guide, not hero), and the piece must still be worth
   reading if the sponsor's name were deleted.

## Blog post

**Beat vocabulary:** headline promise, intro (PAS or APP:
agree-promise-preview), thesis, section beats (H2s), proof/example per
section, synthesis, close/CTA.

**Checks:**
1. **The intro follows a real structure** - Problem-Agitate-Solve or APP
   (Agree with the reader's world, Promise the result, Preview the path) - and
   matches intent: inverted pyramid (answer first) for informational/search
   intent, narrative arc for persuasion and thought leadership. An
   informational post that withholds the answer for suspense is a mismatch.
2. **The headline's promise is fulfilled on the first screen or explicitly
   scheduled** ("here's what we'll cover").
3. **Skim test:** reading only the H2s should convey the thesis. If the
   subheads are labels ("Background", "Our approach") instead of claims, flag
   it.
4. **BUT/THEREFORE between sections.** Each section must earn the next; a post
   whose sections could be shuffled is a listicle wearing an essay's clothes -
   fine if it *claims* to be a listicle, a finding if it doesn't.
5. **Payoff density:** no section without a takeaway, example, or number.
