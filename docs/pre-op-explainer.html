Riverside Growth · HubSpot data concept

The Pre-Op, explained.

A Pre-Op is the top-of-funnel object that tracks Riverside's first engagement with a prospect: one booked intro meeting, one engagement cycle. It's the record every meeting, MQL, SQL, and BD/AE attribution number is counted from, before anything becomes a real opportunity.

Lives in HubSpot as a Deal record 3 pipelines: Agency · EU · Enterprise Promotes 1:1 to a Deal
01

What a Pre-Op actually is

Think of it as the engagement wrapper around a single intro meeting. It exists to make the earliest part of the funnel countable and attributable, long before a deal has real pipeline value.

One meeting, one cycle

Every Pre-Op is tied to a booked intro meeting. One Pre-Op = one engagement cycle with that prospect.

Re-engagement makes a new one

If a prospect was Closed Lost and comes back, a fresh Pre-Op is created with an incremented cycle count, and the old one isn't reused.

Promotes, then freezes

When it converts, it promotes to exactly one Deal (always 1:1) and the Pre-Op record is frozen, with no further edits.

02

The lifecycle

A Pre-Op moves through a small set of stages. Two are terminal; one is a temporary holding pen it can climb back out of. Width below suggests the funnel narrowing toward a promoted deal.

Meeting Booked

Entry point. The Pre-Op is created the moment an intro meeting is scheduled.

Active

Follow-Up

The AE is actively working the prospect after the intro meeting.

Active

Action Required

No-show, unclear next step, or needs attention. Temporary, and can move back to Follow-Up or other active stages.

Holding

Promoted to Deal

Converted. A linked Deal is created via Associated Deal ID. Record frozen.

Terminal

Closed Lost

Didn't convert. Triggers a new cycle if the prospect re-engages later.

Terminal

Booked is not the same as took place. A meeting "took place" only when Intro Meeting Complete Date exists and Intro Meeting Status = Completed. A no-show or cancel with a complete date did not happen, and is not an SQL.

03

Three pipelines, not one

Pre-Ops are split by market. These are not identical copies: they differ in both stages and fields. Any report should confirm which pipeline(s) it means, or query all three.

04

The fields that matter

Dozens of fields hang off a Pre-Op. These are the ones that decide how it's counted, attributed, and qualified.

Last Touch SourceThe single most important attribution field: Inbound Outbound Customer Success. Set once at creation and locked. Use the Pre-Op's field, never the Contact's, because the product overwrites the contact one.
Deal Owner (AE)The Account Executive responsible. Primary ownership field. The current owner at report time gets all credit, with no original-owner tracking.

05

How the KPIs are defined

These definitions are exact. Every meeting, MQL, SQL, and conversion number derives from the same fields and rules, so don't improvise alternatives.

MQL

Marketing Qualified Lead

Pre-Op WHERE Last Touch Source = Inbound

That's the whole definition. An MQL is simply an inbound-sourced Pre-Op, with no extra qualification.

SQL

Sales Qualified Lead

Pre-Op WHERE Intro Meeting Complete Date IS NOT NULL AND Intro Meeting Status = Completed

The intro meeting actually took place, regardless of source (inbound, outbound, or CS). The qualifying event is the completed meeting, not how the lead arrived.

Meetings Booked

Top-of-funnel volume

count(Pre-Op) across Agency + EU + Enterprise

For precise counts, add Intro Meeting Create Date IS NOT NULL to exclude rare manual Pre-Ops.

Conversion Rate

Booked → completed

Completed Meetings ÷ Booked Meetings

Sliceable by AE, BD, pipeline, source, or time period.

06

Outbound & BD attribution

Outbound credit needs two conditions: a source and an owner. Miss either and the meeting falls into an unattributed gap.

Valid BD meeting counted

Last Touch Source = Outbound and BD Owner is populated. Both must be true for a BD to get credit.

Non-BD Outbound gap

Outbound source but BD Owner empty. Currently unattributed, a known gap that still needs addressing.

07

Common pitfalls

The mistakes that quietly corrupt Pre-Op reporting.

×

Booked ≠ completed. A no-show or canceled meeting is not a completed meeting and not an SQL.

×

BD Owner alone ≠ outbound. You need Outbound source and a populated BD Owner.

×

Don't forget the pipeline filter. Three pipelines exist, so confirm which, or query all three.

×

Pipelines aren't identical. Agency, EU, and Enterprise differ in stages and fields.

×

Action Required isn't terminal. It's a holding stage; Pre-Ops return to active from there.

×

Raw Score (AI) is reporting-only. Never use it for routing or automation.

×

Credit the current owner. After an AE transfer, the current Deal Owner gets the credit, not the original.

×

Use the Pre-Op's Last Touch Source. The Contact's version gets overwritten by product events.
