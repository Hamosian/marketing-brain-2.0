The marketing second brain, running as an OS

One compiled, version-controlled knowledge layer for Riverside Marketing, plus the agent layer that operates on it. Every teammate's Claude session boots from the same brain: systems, boards, channels, conventions, and tribal knowledge.

Three layers, one map, one flywheel. Context loads progressively, requests route through /marketing-brain to specialist sub-agents, and every learning is committed back by PR so the next session is smarter than the last.

STEP 01

Load the map

CLAUDE.md is always loaded: team identity, routing tables, directives. Everything else stays on disk until a task needs it.

STEP 02

Route the request

/marketing-brain classifies the ask, pulls the right context, and delegates to domain sub-agents with live access to HubSpot, Omni, monday, and Slack.

STEP 03

Close the loop

Synthesis lands as decision, owner, evidence, next action. Work state hits monday and Slack; /retro commits the learning back to the brain.

Why a second brain

Stop re-deriving context. Compile once, keep current.

Board IDs, channel conventions, HubSpot pipeline quirks, attribution rules, who owns what: without the brain, all of it lives in decks, Slack threads, and people's heads. Every new teammate, and every AI session, has to re-derive it from scratch. The marketing-brain repo fixes that with one compiled, reviewed, always-current context base that Claude boots from.

"Give the AI a map, not a library. Claude gets exactly the context it needs for each task, nothing more." PHILOSOPHY.md, on progressive disclosure
For everyone Why it matters: a messy or missing context base makes AI confidently wrong. A clean one makes it an operating partner that already knows the boards, the channels, the pipelines, and the rules of engagement. How: knowledge lives as pointers, not copies. Dashboards, boards, and CRM views stay the source of truth; the repo holds the map to them.

The loop

Why it self-improves, and why that matters

Most tooling depreciates the day you adopt it. The brain appreciates: every novel workflow, every correction, every discovered platform quirk feeds back in as a reviewed PR. The next session starts from a sharper baseline, for everyone at once.

STEP 01

Capture

After a novel workflow, /retro extracts the durable learning. /curious-intern interviews teammates to fill gaps before they bite.

STEP 02

Commit

The learning is routed to the right file: system doc, skill, reference, or the map, and opened as a PR. CI lints frontmatter, placeholders, and repo hygiene.

STEP 03

Serve

Merged knowledge loads progressively in every teammate's next session. Deterministic automation mirrors it to the wiki and refreshes the knowledge graph.

STEP 04

Learn

/health-check flags staleness and drift. Dead skills get pruned so routing stays sharp. The map stays thin; the depth stays fresh.

capture → commit → serve → learn ↺ every cycle widens the gap between "has the brain" and "doesn't"

For leaders Why it matters: the repo is the department asset the next leader inherits, not a wiki nobody opens. It survives tool changes and staff changes because the knowledge is versioned, reviewed, and owned by the team. How: make the retro non-negotiable after novel work. That single habit is the whole flywheel.

Risks · Anti-patterns · Guardrails

What could go wrong, and what already prevents it

Each failure mode has a mitigation baked into the system, not a policy doc. These are also the questions to push on when extending the brain to a new sub-org.

Stale docs

Claude answers confidently from a doc that no longer matches reality. A messy brain is worse than none.

GuardrailEvery system doc carries a last-reviewed date on line 1. /health-check audits staleness, unfilled placeholders, and template drift.

Copies instead of pointers

Live data pasted into the repo drifts from the source and creates false confidence.

GuardrailThe content boundary rule: if it breaks when the platform changes, it belongs in that platform. The repo holds IDs, links, and maps only.

Map bloat

CLAUDE.md grows until every session wastes its context window before work starts.

GuardrailProgressive disclosure: detail lives in systems/, references/, and skill knowledge/ files that load only when a task needs them.

Knowledge trapped in memory

A learning saved to one person's memory helps exactly one person and can't be reviewed.

GuardrailHard rule: team and system knowledge goes in the repo via PR. Memory is for individual preferences only.

Unreviewed writes to live systems

An agent posts to Slack, edits HubSpot, or mutates a board without a human intending it.

GuardrailConfirmation required before any mutating call, unless a specific automation skill was invoked. Every Slack message carries the Marketing OS agent footer.

Wrong data, high confidence

Ad-hoc SQL quietly disagrees with the analytics team's definitions.

GuardrailRivermind first for every data question. Fallbacks to Omni or raw SQL are explicit, and the coverage gap gets reported back to analytics.

Skill rot

Dead or drifting skills pollute routing and trigger the wrong workflow.

GuardrailCI lints every skill's frontmatter on PR. The RALPH lifecycle (review, audit, learn, prune, handoff) kills skills that no longer earn their place.

Wiki drift

The browsable mirror falls out of sync with the repo and someone trusts the wrong copy.

GuardrailMerge automation regenerates the wiki and knowledge graph on every merge to main. The repo is always the source of truth regardless.
Reference

Glossary & FAQ

What's the difference between the Marketing Brain and the Marketing OS?
The brain is the knowledge layer: the repo itself, with its map, references, system docs, and skills. The OS is the operating layer that runs on it: /marketing-brain plus the sub-agent registry, which classifies requests, loads context, delegates, synthesizes, and closes the loop. Same repo, two roles.
Why a git repo and not a Notion wiki?
Version control, PR review, and CI are the guardrails. A wiki can't lint a skill's frontmatter, block an unfilled placeholder, or show you a diff of what the AI learned. The team still gets a browsable wiki: deterministic automation generates one from the repo on every merge.
What loads when I start a session?
Only CLAUDE.md, the map. It tells Claude what exists and where. References, system docs, and skill knowledge load on demand when the task touches them. That's progressive disclosure: sessions start instant and stay lean.
Do the specialist agents touch our live systems?
No. Only the routing-layer skill-agents hold live access to HubSpot, Omni, monday, Slack, and the ad platforms. Specialists receive the pulled data plus a harmonized Riverside context block, do the deep domain pass, and hand back. Any mutating action surfaces for human confirmation.
How does new knowledge get in?
Through the flywheel. /retro captures a learning after a novel workflow and routes it to the right file as a PR. /curious-intern works the other direction: it interviews you to extract what's in your head before it's needed. Nobody writes big docs from scratch; that's the anti-pattern.
Who owns this, and who is it for?
It originated in Growth and Marketing Operations, and that's where operational depth is greatest today. It serves all of Riverside Marketing: Growth, Brand, Marketing, AI Marketing, and Growth Initiatives are onboarding into the same system, and every skill and doc in it is shared.
What if the docs are wrong?
Fix them in the same motion. Edits to the map, systems, references, and skills are ordinary PRs, linted by CI. If you found the error mid-task, that's exactly what /retro is for. Staleness that nobody noticed is what /health-check is for.
