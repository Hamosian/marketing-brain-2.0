# Riverside's new story (2026)

Source: [Riverside's new story deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Dp3LoSEKAhDBtDnUABUaKlGsyleh_-a9XmEsdBM2K_k/edit) (Product Marketing team, under Sivan Mazuz). The strategic narrative work behind the 2026 repositioning. Use this when framing top-of-story copy: homepage, about page, manifestos, sales narratives, brand campaigns.

## The evolution

| Year | Story |
|------|-------|
| 2021 | Recording studio from anywhere |
| 2025 | The AI-powered all-in-one platform for creating, hosting, repurposing, and distributing content |
| 2026 | Media company in your browser |

## Why tell a new story

1. "Studio" under-describes us.
2. "All-in-one" is accurate but generic.
3. We describe features, not the end result.

## The research base

70+ power user interviews, 90+ community member voices, 50+ organic social posts analyzed, 20+ Gong calls. Caveat noted in the deck: these are power users, active community members and closed-won deals. They love us, so the signal is strong on what we do right but quieter on churn and early friction.

What users say (full evidence in `community-voice.md` and `power-user-interviews.md`):

- "Riverside saved me about 5 hours of each episode (from 7 hours to 2)."
- "Magic Clips really helps and has made us money."
- "Riverside makes me, a one woman show podcaster, look like I have a team of producers and editors."
- "It turned a 15 IG live into 15 pieces of content for me."
- "I think it's going to be the Canva of podcasting."
- Business buyers emphasize quality plus self-serve speed: "Even if Zoom allowed me to make these short clips, my audio and video quality is not what we want to repurpose." (BMC Software)

What users do NOT say (gaps to be aware of): nothing about "AI vs human content", they do not self-identify as "a creator/brand", and they do not say "I came to you to grow my audience."

## Three framings

Users talk about Riverside in three registers, and the story can be told in each:

| Register | User language | Story frame |
|----------|--------------|-------------|
| What it does | "Everything happens in one place." "I don't need 5 tools anymore." | All-in-one workflow replacement |
| What I get | "One recording became 15 pieces of content." "It gave me hours back." | Changed math of content creation |
| How I feel | "It made me feel like I have a team." "I can finally enjoy creating." | Creating feels like creating again |

### The three-part system (how they live together)

- **Product positioning (what we do):** "Where great convos become great content." Lives on: homepage, decks, sales pages, organic social, product marketing.
- **Brand promise (why we do it, how they feel):** "How creating content should feel." Lives on: about page, founder narrative, hiring brand, social mission, manifesto.
- **Brand USP (what they get that no one else gives):** "Record, edit, grow. All in one place." Lives on: acquisition ads, hooks, influencers.

## Competitive positioning

The market splits into three groups, and Riverside's opportunity is owning the intersection as the end-to-end content platform:

- **Production tools** (Descript, CapCut, Restream, Opus, Streamyard, VEED, Zencastr, Podcastle): own AI, ease, quality.
- **Creation tools** (Descript, Canva): own AI, ease, quality.
- **Growth platforms** (Beehiiv, Substack, Kajabi): own growth and money.

Competitor messaging is mostly about what the product does. Riverside's unique advantage: unlike most competitors, we own the creation step (the recording itself).

## How to treat AI in the story

From **AI as promise** ("we are an AI-powered platform") to **AI as proof** ("AI is what makes the change possible"). AI is the force multiplier that enables the outcome, not the headline identity. This matches how users talk: they never lead with AI, they lead with time saved and output multiplied.
