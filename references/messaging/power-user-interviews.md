# Power user interviews: synthesis

Source: [Power users interviews doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/12HZMrimwTb8b3-UrX1qLBV6R7vFzqHB2xehK_ETp0qI/edit) (Product Marketing team, under Sivan Mazuz). Interview notes for roughly 55 top users, distilled here for messaging, positioning, and campaign work. The source doc holds the full per-user notes.

## What the source document is

A working research doc from Riverside's user research outreach (calls run by team members including Ann and Kendall). Most users are captured twice: once as raw call notes and once as an AI-standardized interview summary following a shared template (background, workflow, feedback, likes, struggles, goals, feature requests, memorable quotes). The interviews probe: who the creator is, their end-to-end workflow, why they chose Riverside, what they compare it to, pain points and feature requests, and their goals. The doc skews positive because it samples loyal power users, but it is candid about friction and includes several at-risk accounts.

## Who was interviewed

- Solo and duo podcasters across every niche: mental health, faith and spirituality, sports, comedy, pop culture, legal, finance, franchising, affiliate marketing, startups.
- Business users who treat the podcast as a lead pipeline or brand channel: coaching, Spanish-language content for Latino construction owners, brand podcasts (Ripstop by the Roll), community media (Startup CPG, 3 episodes/week plus webinars), a 12-person wellness company producing 500+ client podcasts (Balancing Life's Issues).
- Professional producers and agencies: Multitude Productions (25 client shows), producers for coaches, German and UK agencies, 20-year podcast studios.
- Broadcast-grade users: a Radio New Zealand-trained engineer running a weekly live show, church livestreaming.
- International voices: India, Cameroon and the African diaspora, Nigeria, France, Germany, UK, New Zealand, Canada.

## Why they chose Riverside (recurring decision drivers)

1. **Recording quality and reliability**, driven by local per-track recording with progressive upload. "Unbeatable." One user has "never had a single ruined audio upload." Riverside "always finds a way to recover the recording."
2. **Escape from Zoom.** Zoom is the universal negative anchor; nearly everyone frames the switch as Zoom-to-Riverside.
3. **Tool consolidation.** The record-edit-clip-publish all-in-one story is the single most repeated value narrative. One user replaced three paid tools (~EUR 60/month).
4. **Time savings, often quantified.** "Riverside saved me about 5 hours of each episode (from 7 hours to 2)." "It takes longer to export than it does to edit."
5. **Product velocity.** Users notice weekly shipping and treat it as a loyalty reason; wished-for features appear "within two to three weeks."
6. **Community and support.** Users switched from Descript partly because "the community is a huge selling point." Human, fast support is repeatedly praised.
7. **Price-to-value.** "I can't believe you're not charging $1,000 a year."

## Feature-level signal (roughly ranked by frequency)

- **Magic Clips**: the most-mentioned feature by far. Loved as a time-saver and even a revenue driver ("Magic Clips really helps and has made us money"). Recurrent asks: fewer but smarter clips, trend awareness, guest-only clips, exact clip lengths, better multi-speaker handling.
- **Magic Audio**: second most cited. "Saves my life because none of my guests have good mics." It replaced a human audio editor for one producer. Gaps: adjustable intensity, EQ and room-tone control (Adobe Enhanced Speech is the benchmark), occasional "electronic" artifacts.
- **Text-based and transcript editing**: called a "game changer" repeatedly. Transcripts feed books, blogs, ChatGPT workflows, and accessibility. Reliability of text deletions is a known sore spot.
- **Local recording plus fast upload**: the foundational differentiator, especially versus Zoom and for guests with bad internet.
- **Filler-word removal and Cut Fluff**: loved, though pros find it clips words too tightly and prefer manual control.
- **Smart layouts and auto speaker switching**: widely used and liked, with requests for content-aware cuts and auto-zoom.
- **AI show notes, titles, keywords, chapters**: heavily used but consistently rated robotic and too generic. Almost every power user exports the transcript to ChatGPT or Claude with custom prompts instead. Co-Creator awareness is low and trust is mixed.
- **Hosting and direct publishing**: strong anticipation. Direct YouTube and Spotify publish is loved when it works; users want full parity (thumbnails, metadata, scheduling), dynamic ad insertion, and Buzzsprout-style analytics before they migrate.
- Also praised: markers, teleprompter, music library, captions, translation and dubbing ("absolutely blown away, wild tech"), two-mic in-person support.

## The competitive frame users actually live in

- **Recording rivals**: Zoom, StreamYard, Zencastr, SquadCast, Podcastle, EVMUX, Descript. Riverside wins on quality, tracks, and breadth.
- **Clips rival**: Opus Clip is the reference point for Magic Clips, mostly on "easy share" (direct posting, templates, scheduling), not clip quality.
- **Editing rivals users still fall back to**: Premiere, Final Cut, DaVinci Resolve, Audition, Pro Tools, Audacity, Filmora, CapCut. The gap is DAW-grade precision: frame-accurate cuts, music fades by decibel, waveform detail, overlays.
- **Adjacent stack**: Canva (thumbnails), ChatGPT (titles, notes, articles), Buffer and Later (social scheduling), vidIQ (YouTube SEO), Buzzsprout, Transistor, Libsyn, Megaphone (hosting and monetization), PodMatch (guest sourcing).

## Segments and how the story differs

- **Solo passion and mission creators** (largest group): time-poor, do everything themselves, emotionally attached. Care about ease, Magic tools, cost. Their real pain is promotion and growth, not production. Many hate marketing outright ("I'm terrible at promotion, I just enjoy the conversations").
- **Business and lead-gen users**: the podcast is a pipeline, not a product. They measure leads, not listeners (one sent a 6K proposal off one episode; another gets clients from clips, not the feed). Message: professional output with near-zero production overhead.
- **Producers and agencies**: multi-show, multi-client. Value reliability, transcripts, client-friendly guest UX. Blocked by account structure: single studio limits, the Pro-to-Business pricing cliff, no folders, password sharing with editors. They want a mid-tier team plan and editor seats.
- **Brand and B2B media teams**: webinars, compliance (HIPAA, 508/ADA, SSO), attendee moderation, capacity. High revenue stakes make reliability failures existential; strong partnership appetite.
- **Broadcast-grade power users**: want producer green-room controls during live shows, gain staging, loudness standards, DAW-level editing. Open to AI only if edits are inaudible and control is preserved ("You should not be able to hear an edit").
- **Journalists**: draw a hard line between drudgery-removing automation (acceptable) and content-altering or generative AI (reputational risk). Positioning AI as "a Photoshop filter, not Photoshop" matters for this segment.

## Pain points, churn risks, and wish list

In rough order of frequency and heat:

1. **No native social publishing and scheduling.** The most requested capability. Users cobble together Buffer, Later, Opus Clip; posting clips takes 30 minutes or more per episode. One-button clip-to-social with per-platform presets would displace multiple subscriptions.
2. **Editor precision.** Timeline zoom, frame-accurate cuts, music fades by decibel, reusable overlay templates, custom fonts below Enterprise. Several users say they would go 90-100% in-platform if this closed.
3. **Live streaming reliability** (acute churn risk). One user invites ~500 people weekly and "probably three quarters of our shows don't go live." Also: unclear "you're live" signals, Instagram and TikTok setup friction, green-room and producer controls.
4. **Bugs that erode trust**: screen-share desync on long sessions (one user actively considering switching), muted audio inside exported shorts, Magic Clips caption resets, black screens on playback, false "internet too slow" errors. "It's hard to get excited about a new feature when you're worried the old stuff will still work."
5. **Pricing and packaging gaps**: Pro-to-Business cliff, team accounts perceived as 10x more expensive, webinar add-on too pricey for mid-stage creators, multi-studio and folder organization missing, renewal and discount confusion (a stated churn moment).
6. **Generic AI copy.** AI titles and show notes lose to users' custom ChatGPT prompts because they lack tone, voice training, and context.
7. **Guest experience edge cases**: Zoom-accustomed and older guests confused by invites, corporate firewalls blocking financial-sector guests, low-bandwidth guests.
8. **Wish-list themes with strategic weight**: monetization marketplace and ad exchange (asked for independently by at least four users), a vetted Riverside editor marketplace ("I'd pay $40-50 per episode"), audience analytics and benchmarks, niche growth education, live transcription, 508/HIPAA compliance.

## Strategically notable for messaging and positioning

- **Lead with consolidation plus time math.** The strongest proof points are quantified time savings and subscriptions cancelled. "Replace your Zoom, Descript, Opus, and Buffer stack" mirrors how users already narrate their journey.
- **Zoom is the enemy in the customer's own words.** Anti-Zoom comparative copy is pre-validated by user language.
- **Reliability is the brand's load-bearing wall.** Local recording, upload recovery, and never-lost-a-track stories are the trust foundation; every reliability bug spends that capital. Be careful not to overpromise on flaky surfaces (live streaming).
- **Product velocity is itself a message.** Users experience Riverside as a partner that ships their wishes within weeks.
- **The user's biggest unsolved problem is growth, not production.** Content about promotion, YouTube strategy, and trends builds outsized goodwill; a trends newsletter, niche growth tracks, and creator communities were explicitly requested.
- **Advocacy is organic and hot.** Multiple users convert friends, decline referral fees, offer testimonials, and request affiliate links. There is a ready-made ambassador and case-study bench in the source doc.
- **Position AI as assistive, not generative.** Across segments, the winning frame is AI that removes drudgery while the creator keeps taste and control. Eye contact correction and AI B-roll read as "too AI" to this audience; filler removal, leveling, layouts, and transcripts read as magic.
- **Segment-specific stories beat one-size-fits-all**: quality and craft for podcasters, lead-gen ROI for business users, scale-with-a-small-team for agencies and brands, broadcast control for pros.
