Read this first - three things that change how the numbers should be read
  • This is a recovery quarter, not a growth quarter. Non-brand organic has been declining since the December 2025 core update, compounded by the riverside.fm to riverside.com migration and brand-entity ambiguity. Q2 is measured against that, and the Algorythmic audit was commissioned in July to find the root cause.
  • Attribution basis: best_attributed_first_visit_channel_group (the July 2026 first-visit fix). Every comparison period is restated on this same basis, so the numbers here will not match older reports built on the pre-fix field. 58.8% of all signups remain unattributed, so every channel figure is a floor, not a total.
  • Search Console has no valid year-over-year comparison. The riverside.com property holds only ~17 days of data in May-July 2025 because of the migration. Every GSC comparison here is versus the prior quarter (Feb-Apr 2026). Business results from Snowflake are user-level and unaffected, so those do carry a true YoY.

Organic revenue grew year over year while organic traffic fell sharply. That is not a contradiction, it is the whole story of the quarter: the channel is being rebuilt on a different foundation. Classic non-brand search is roughly half what it was, and the gap has been filled by AI surfaces and by brand demand converting harder.

Total organic new MRR of $194,662 was up 8.7% on the same quarter last year. Underneath that, organic non-brand fell 42% and organic LLM rose 227%. Riverside is now the most-visible brand in its category across AI assistants and the second most-cited domain overall, behind only YouTube. At the same time, non-brand impressions halved versus the prior quarter, and the commercial "best podcast recording software" keyword cluster fell out of the top 10.

The number most likely to be challenged

Organic LLM looks like the hero of the quarter, and partly it is. But only 25% of Q2 LLM signups came from an observed AI referral visit. The other 75% are onboarding-survey self-reports where someone typed that they found us via ChatGPT or Gemini. A year ago that split was 63% observed / 37% self-reported. Measured LLM revenue still nearly doubled YoY ($6.6k to $12.3k), which is real, but it is a fraction of the $62.8k headline. Section 4 breaks this out rather than burying it.

The shape matters more than the total. Non-brand bottomed in June at 20,172 clicks and recovered 23% in July to 24,810. One month is not a trend, but it is the first month-on-month increase since the decline began, and it lines up with the Project Polaris technical work going live.

Top-3 presence nearly doubled while the top 10 emptied out. Thirty-seven keywords slid from page one into 11-20. The losing set is coherent rather than random: it is almost entirely the commercial cluster, "best podcast software", "best remote podcast recording software", "record podcast software", "podcast video software", all falling from positions 5-10 into the high 20s. On AI Overviews, keywords showing an AIO rose from 212 to 241, and the count where Riverside appears inside the Overview went from 4 to 12.

Needs verification before this goes to anyone else

Ahrefs records the keyword "riverside" at position 1 on 1 May and position 12 on 31 July, taking its traffic estimate from 19,397 to zero. Search Console does not corroborate a collapse of that scale: brand clicks fell 6% over the quarter, not 100%. This is most likely SERP volatility or a rank-tracker artifact tied to the brand-entity ambiguity Abel flagged in May, where Google conflates Riverside and riverside.fm. I would not report it as a lost brand ranking without checking the live SERP. Strip that one keyword out and non-brand traffic for the tracked set more than doubled, from 5,113 to 11,622.

Publishing volume: the quarter's quietest number

90 unique URLs were touched across Q2, up from 66 in the prior quarter. But only 4 were new pages. The other 86 were updates to existing pages, and 33 of those were author and category pages rather than editorial content. May alone accounted for 48 of the 93 rows, after which output halved. The team spent Q2 repairing rather than expanding, which is the right call during a recovery, but it should be stated plainly rather than presented as publishing throughput.

What blocked us

  • Localisation is structurally blocked, not behind schedule. German SEOs cannot edit localised pages directly because Crowdin is the source of truth and overrides Webflow. Changes have to be passed through Google Sheets. French went live-ready in SEO terms and then sat waiting on a Webflow publishing issue.
  • Pruned pages are still discoverable in blog catalogue and search result pages despite being 301'd or 410'd, flagged 14 July and unresolved.
  • A 302 redirect loop on the language selector, found 17 July, largely invisible to crawlers but a genuine problem.
  • FAQ schema has no CMS-level solution; every option requires server-side development.
  • Competing priorities: Q3 planning and the blog audit displaced the University redirect mapping, and side tasks displaced category page work.
Section 5

What I would take from this

  • The commercial keyword cluster is the priority, not blog volume. "Best podcast recording software" and its neighbours falling from the top 10 to the high 20s is worth more revenue than anything else on the list. That cluster is where non-brand money actually comes from.
  • The product page rewrites need to ship or be killed. A full quarter of briefs and content with zero pages live is the clearest process failure in this report. The bottleneck is design and build capacity, not SEO.
  • Non-brand impressions halving is the leading indicator to watch. Clicks bottomed in June and recovered in July, but impressions tell us whether we are eligible to compete at all. If impressions do not follow clicks upward in August, the July recovery was noise.
  • German needs a non-brand diagnosis. Zero non-brand conversions across two quarters in our fifth-largest click market is a specific, solvable problem.
  • Fix the LLM measurement before the next report. Running at 75% self-reported attribution means we cannot tell how much of the biggest growth story is real. This is worth a conversation with Danielle.
  • Ask about Microsoft. Half a million crawls, zero citations, ramping 42x. Either something is broken or we are excluded from something.
Appendix

Method and known gaps

  • Periods. Q2 FY26 = 1 May to 31 July 2026. Prior quarter = 1 Feb to 30 April 2026. YoY = 1 May to 31 July 2025.
  • Attribution. best_attributed_first_visit_channel_group from analytics.bi.marketing_funnel_analysis_wo_prediction, applied identically to all periods. Revenue is counted by subscription activation date, not signup date, so July is not penalised for immature cohorts. 58.8% of signups remain unattributed; all channel figures are floors.
  • Brand rule. A query is brand if it contains "riverside", "river", or any phonetic or typo variant (reverside, riveside, riversde, riversside, riverfm, river transcribe, riverstream and roughly 190 others). Product words alone never make a query brand. Including bare "river" moved 2,338 Q2 clicks out of non-brand; 168 distinct queries were swept up, overwhelmingly keyboard typos of "riverside" plus a genuine navigational cluster ("river transcribe", "river podcast", "riverfm") sitting at positions 1-4 with 20-90% CTR. Fourteen genuine false positives were excluded by hand, including "riverbed"-style matches inside "transcriber" and "drivers", River Indie e-bikes, and Riverdale TV-show queries, together worth 10 Q2 clicks. Five ambiguous "riverdale" queries (39 clicks) were kept as brand because they rank on riverside.com product pages at 25-50% CTR. Versus the narrowest possible brand rule, these corrections reduce reported non-brand by 16.3%.
  • GSC coverage. Google anonymises the query on 22% of Q2 clicks and 40% of impressions, so brand plus non-brand does not equal the total. Nothing has been extrapolated to close that gap. The GSC API used here accepts only rolling windows, so calendar months were isolated by exact nested-window subtraction; click and impression figures are exact integers, average position is impression-weighted and carries ±0.06.
  • No GSC year-over-year. The riverside.com property holds roughly 17 days of data in the May-July 2025 window because of the domain migration. Any YoY GSC figure would be an artifact.
  • Profound bots. The API mislabels its own metric order; verified by isolating each metric that position one is crawls and position two is citations. Provider and bot-type totals disagree by 640,408 crawls, cause unresolved; provider totals are used here.
  • Ahrefs. Rank tracker responses cap at 250 rows, so figures were built from verified disjoint partitions that cross-check to 569 tagged keywords. Traffic values are rounded integers as displayed in the Ahrefs UI.
  • Not established. Nothing in Section 4 is claimed to have caused a metric change in Section 1 or 3. Q2 work and Q2 results are reported separately because a single quarter cannot establish causation for pages that take three to six months to mature.
