# Video Creative Brief Templates

Two Google Doc templates for briefing a new marketing video. Owned by Brand
(Raz Messing's org - creative direction, design, motion, performance video).
**Anyone opening a new video creative brief must start from one of these two
templates** rather than a blank doc or a copy of an old brief - the fields
below are what a designer/editor needs to start work without a follow-up
meeting.

The Google Docs are the source of truth (they may be edited over time); the
structure below is a snapshot for quick reference. If the two disagree, the
Doc wins.

## Which template

| Use... | When the video is... |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Creative Video Brief** | Narrative/brand-led - has to persuade someone who may not know Riverside yet. Built around a problem/promise arc (who they are, what they believe, why they should believe us). |
| **Motion Demo Video Brief** | Product/feature-led - shows a specific feature or workflow working. Built around a demo flow (exact sequence of product moments) rather than a persuasion arc. |

If a video does both (a launch video that both demos a feature and makes a
brand claim), default to **Creative Video Brief** - it's the stricter of the
two and its demo section ("Why should they believe us?") covers the same
ground as the Motion Demo template's product flow.

## 1. Creative Video Brief

Source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1CiPRkspaWJG8Ee29an88NiqeLo9WwIFXa8oWd7HZgJ0/edit ("[Template] Creative video brief", owned by Raz Messing, last modified 2026-08-26; checked 2026-09-24). The `17bpQApat7n90Lt4JbjkJDZiO-bK5M6aEu8kbbBDKoJw` doc this used to link is a `[Project]` copy with the same structure, not the template (`.claude/skills/video-project-intake/knowledge/board-schema.md`).

```
# [Project] Creative Video Brief

Owner:

## Context
What is this video about and why are we making it now?
E.g. Major editor upgrade, we want to use the launch to position it as the
editor built for conversations.

Deadline:

## Who are we talking to?
Be specific on their intent, experience and where they are in their journey
with Riverside. (Awareness level matrix: see reference link in the source doc)
E.g. people who had experience with conversations editing and know the pain,
but not necessarily aware of Riverside or its editing capabilities.

## What is their problem?
E.g. They hate editing their recordings.

Why does that problem suck?!
E.g. It takes so much time and energy. Even more than recording the
conversation.

## How can we help them?
E.g. We built an editor for conversations to make it so much easier than a
standard video editor.

## What do we want them to think & feel when they finish watching?
E.g. Think: "Oh, editing is frustrating because I'm not using the right
editor for my content." Feel: confidence - "this is the editor I want."

If organic video, add social captions here.

## Why should they believe us?
What does the viewer see Riverside do to prove that? Walk through the exact
sequence of product moments that prove the main promise. Be specific about
features shown.
1.
2.
3.
Product: [Figma Link] [product demo]

## Distribution
How does this get in front of the audience?
Paid or organic:
Main channel: (YouTube / LinkedIn / X / Instagram / TikTok / other)
Aspect ratio / specs: (e.g. 16:9 for YT, 9:16 for stories, 1:1 for feed)

## References (optional)
List 2-3 specific references. For each one, say exactly what you're
borrowing - the pacing, the hook structure, the graphic treatment, the tone.
Not "vibes like this" - what specifically.
- [Title / link] - Why: ...
- [Title / link] - Why: ...
- [Title / link] - Why: ...

## What success looks like
One sentence on what this video needs to do - a metric, a behavior, a
feeling.

## What this is NOT
Guard rails. What tone, claim, or angle should we avoid?
E.g: avoid "AI doing it all for you" sentiment - they appreciate control.
```

## How to start a new brief

1. Pick the template using the table above.
2. Make a copy of the linked Google Doc (File > Make a copy) - don't start
   from a blank doc or an old brief, since fields drift out of sync that way.
3. Fill in every section; leave `[bracketed placeholders]` visibly unfilled
   (not deleted) if the answer isn't known yet, so a reviewer can see what's
   still open rather than assuming it was considered and left out.
4. Link the finished brief from wherever the request is tracked (a monday
   ticket, a Slack thread) rather than pasting its contents elsewhere.
