# What in a Ziff brief predicted a bad meeting (batch 1, read 2026-09-19)

Nir's question: which briefs should never have been booked, judged by what the AE found on the call. This file is the history behind the flag rules in `SKILL.md`. It is analysis for Nir; it is not vendor feedback until he asks for that.

## The data

47 unique briefs (5 August to 17 September 2026, the whole period Charles has emailed handover documents), 22 of them with a next-day outcome: 5 qualified, 9 weak, 8 unqualified. Outcome read from the Gong summary and the Pre-Op stage; AEs rarely move the stage, so the Gong summary carries most of it. The three agreed discovery questions start on 18 September, so none of these briefs is graded on them.

## What separated the eight unqualified briefs

| Signal in the brief | On unqualified briefs | On weak | On qualified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title is not a marketing, content, brand, comms, video or production owner | 5 of 8 | 0 of 9 | 1 of 5 |
| No decision owner named and no team size given | 3 of 8 | 1 of 9 | 1 of 5 |
| Either of the two | 5 of 8 | 1 of 9 | 1 of 5 |
| One person recording alone on consumer tools | 0 of 8 | 3 of 9 | 0 of 5 |

The five unqualified briefs the title rule catches: a hospitality development manager (ScotRail), a brand experience account manager at an exhibition-space company (Spaceandpeople), a head of UX and design at a newspaper (The Independent), a project marketing manager at a record label (Nettwerk), and an interim "marketing leader" with no programme (Metro Wallcoverings). The one qualified brief it wrongly catches is FiveTran, a regional field marketer who told Charles she was not the owner and then forwarded the invite to the owner.

The three unqualified briefs nothing catches: ASM Global (venue teams of one or two people, small budgets; the brief read as a normal marketing programme), OnePoint Patient Care (the prospect said the current setup works), Smoltek (pre-revenue, took the meeting because Charles mentioned animations). On paper all three looked ordinary. Two of the three would have been exposed by "what do you use today and are you happy with it" and "is there budget this year", which is why those are in the agreed questions now.

## What did not separate them

- **How the programme was described.** Rubric points for programme fit averaged 23 on qualified briefs and 19 on unqualified. "Interested in", "exploring", "planning" appear on most briefs of every outcome; the brief format invites that language. Nir's July finding that "planning with no date and no host" never converts is not contradicted, it is simply untested in this batch: the planning-only briefs (Broughton, Daboom, CFH) have no outcome yet.
- **Company size, country, industry, seniority level.** No pattern at 22 rows.
- **Budget.** Almost never asked before 18 September, so no signal either way.
- **The 0-100 score's middle band.** Six of the eight unqualified meetings scored between 53 and 67, next to weak and qualified ones. The score adds up what the brief says; the unqualified briefs were not weaker on paper, they were about the wrong person or silent on ownership.

## Caveats, stated plainly

- Eight unqualified meetings is a small base. The title rule and the owner rule are confirmed on the batch that suggested them (three of the five titles above also appear in Nir's 16 September analysis), so the real test is the next 20 outcomes.
- "Weak" meetings (nine) are not counted as bad here, per Nir's decision on 2026-09-19: a weak meeting had a real programme behind it and the AE can work it.
- Self-serve profiles produced weak meetings, not unqualified ones. They are still a $750 route to a $30-a-month customer, so they carry their own flag.

## What this means for Ziff, when Nir decides to send it

Two questions would have prevented five of the eight bad meetings: is this person the marketing or production owner (or the one who runs the programme), and who decides on video tools and how many people produce. Both are already in the three agreed questions. The feedback is not "book different companies"; it is "answer those two every time, and when the title is outside marketing or production, find the owner before booking."
