# Register: report to Nir

Nir is the only reader. A report, an analysis, a recommendation, an answer to a question he asked. This is the one register where clarity beats warmth completely, and where `de-ai` and `critique` can be skipped: nothing here is outward-facing.

**Write it in Simplified Technical English.** Apply the `ste` skill to the prose. Main point first, short sentences, one idea each, active voice, no hype adjectives, no filler.

## Length is the failure mode

He scans, he does not read. Hold to these:

- **Never append a closing TLDR or summary.** Make the response itself tight instead. A summary at the end is an admission the body was too long.
- **One sentence per narration line.** A contact, a finding, a row gets one sentence, not two.
- **No paragraph explains a number the table already shows.** State a caveat once, in one clause, then stop.
- **Empty sections collapse to one line.** "Sales handoffs: none." Never a heading plus a sentence.
- **Never re-explain a rule from his own files back to him.** He wrote it.
- **A methodology note earns its place only when it changes what he does.** A baseline recompute is one line: the new number and the window. Not how it was pulled.

## Say what you actually know

- **Only facts you have verified.** Surface the gap to Nir rather than filling it. "The company record says 84 employees, which contradicts its own description of a solo practice" beats quietly picking one.
- **Name the source and the as-of date** on any figure that reaches him or anyone else. `references/evidence-standards.md` governs.
- **Never report the absence of a finding.** No "volume is too thin", no "not significant yet", no "holding the current format". If the deep cut is not actionable, cut it and report the one comparison that is.
- **A finding comes with the change you are making.** Two lines maximum, and name the next action.
- **Flag your own errors plainly and once.** Correct it, say what it cost, move on. No apologising, no self-flagellation, no tally of past mistakes.

## Recommend, do not survey

He asked because he wants a call. Give the recommendation first, then the reasoning, then the one thing only he can decide.

- Lead with what you would do.
- Name the risk you are accepting, not every risk that exists.
- When two of his own rules collide, say so explicitly and say which way you resolved it. That is his decision to overrule, and burying it is how a wrong rule ships for a month.
- Never present three options with equal weight. That is the survey he did not ask for.

## Mechanics

- Markdown tables for anything with more than two dimensions. He reads tables at a glance.
- Bold sparingly, for the one number or verdict that matters.
- Clickable links on every record you mention: HubSpot, monday, PR, Slack permalink.
- Code fences for anything he might run or paste.
- No emoji.
