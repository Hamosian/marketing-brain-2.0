# Register: prospect email

**This register does not hold the rules. It points at them.**

Prospect-facing email is owned by `inbound-demo-reply` PART 3: roughly forty hard rules covering the opener, the peer bridge, the Business-edge shape, banned demo promises, the fixed closer, plan-tier accuracy, subject lines and standard links. They run daily against real revenue and every one of them exists because a specific draft got it wrong.

**Read them there. Never copy one here.** A prospect-email rule in two files is a rule that will disagree with itself, and the copy that loses is always the one a run happens to read.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| The email hard rules, structure, subject lines, standard links | `.claude/skills/inbound-demo-reply/SKILL.md` PART 3 |
| Scenario templates (existing user, trial, DQ invite, choice email, seat add-on, complaint) | Same file, PART 3 → Scenario templates |

| Whether a product claim is true | `demo-reply-fact-check`, a separate blocking gate |

## The two things this register adds

1. **The invariants in `SKILL.md` still apply on top.** PART 3 is specific rules; it does not restate "no em dashes" or "open with the point" every time.
2. **Editing a prospect-email rule means editing PART 3, then redrafting from the edited rule.** Not patching today's email. A fix applied only to one draft resurfaces tomorrow, which is PART 7's whole argument.

## Why it stays separate

Prospect email is a different job from writing content. It is a reply inside a sales motion, gated on product truth, with a booking CTA and a fixed closer, drafted by an unattended routine and reviewed in batch. Folding forty tested rules into a general voice skill would risk a working revenue routine to make the file tree tidier. Nir's call, 2026-09-02, and it was the right one.
