# 1-1 agenda style - how Nir wants lead agendas built

Loaded by `/chief-of-staff` when Nir asks for a 1-1 agenda / topics list for one of
his leads (e.g. "list of topics and action items for Erika for our 1-1 tomorrow"),
and by every **1-1 pack** the brief builds on a 1-1 day (`knowledge/1-1-pack.md`
owns when a pack is built, what goes in, and the approval and learning loop; this
file owns how the list reads once the content is chosen).
Written after the Erika agenda of 2026-08-31 took three rewrites to land; the rules
below are what he corrected, so follow them on the first draft.

## The deliverable

A numbered list of **action items the lead can prepare against**. Each item is a
short bold title plus one or two lines saying exactly what Nir wants. The lead
should be able to read the list once and know what to bring. It goes through
nik-voice → de-ai before Nir sees it (it is outward-facing prep), and the DM to the
lead still needs his OK on exact wording before scheduling or sending.

## Rules, in the order they were violated

1. **Asks, not descriptions.** "Joto: what the campaign produced, what we learned,
   whether we run it again" - not "Joto: their report plus the Thursday session."
   An item that only names a topic tells the lead nothing.
2. **Nir's priority order, not the board's.** Ignore the Priority column for
   ordering. His pattern: cadence and ownership gaps first (weekly report, who owns
   what), then things he has asked for repeatedly without result, then practical
   shipping failures stated plainly ("we haven't shipped a product page in months,
   it needs to be resolved"). Reports and analysis questions go last, usually as
   "let's book time later in the week" rather than 1-1 material.
3. **No board metadata.** No P-levels, no ETAs, no "Nd late", no source links, no
   Bring:-blocks unless one genuinely clarifies the ask.
4. **Consolidate hard.** Same-theme ledger rows merge into one item: five blog rows
   are one Blog item, three German threads are one German item. Well under 10 items
   total.
5. **State the problem, never the diagnosis.** No speculating about capacity,
   dependencies, or unclear briefs, and no process framing ("this is a process
   conversation, not a performance one" was rejected as nonsense). The practical
   problem is the whole item.
6. **Positive and constructive tone**, including on items the lead has been slow on.
   "Let's close it tomorrow", never "you've been ignoring this".
7. **Check who owns each deliverable.** Nir keeps some pieces himself (he took the
   review replies; QBR slides were his, not Erika's). A deadline that is Nir's own
   gets surfaced to him separately, never folded into the lead's agenda.
8. **No framing questions or strategy asides** unless Nir wrote them himself. His
   bullets are talking points, not an outline to expand.

## Ledger hygiene feeds this (Nir, 2026-08-31)

The reason the first draft sprawled: the ledgers held terse, undefined rows
("trustpilot", "Blog updates") next to unmerged same-topic rows. On the **daily
run**, do not leave those sitting: ask Nir what an undefined row means, write his
answer into the row's Answer column, and propose merging same-topic rows into one.
Clean ledgers make the agenda a grouping exercise instead of an archaeology dig.
