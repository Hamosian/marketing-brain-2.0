# Hook and retention psychology - the first fifteen seconds and the loops after

Reference for critiquing or writing the opening of any feed video: YouTube
scripts, paid social video, product social clips, performance video. Loaded
by `script-doctor` alongside `format-playbooks.md`. Use it to name *why* an
opening loses the room, not just that it does.

**Source:** a public thread by Jacob (@jforjacob), 2026-01-21, as synthesized
in a three-page editorial by Founder Thoughts & Strategies (@mogulinfluence),
2026-09-04. The source itself flags every timing below as a craft heuristic
from practice, not a measured constant. Treat the numbers as calibration, the
mechanisms as the point.

## Five insights, and what each means for our scripts

1. **Write the payoff first and the hook last.** Most creators film the
   content, then bolt a hook on the front. The order that works: the payoff in
   one sentence, the gap between that and what the viewer knows now, the stake
   (what not knowing costs them), then the interrupt, written last and placed
   first. *For us:* a brief that can't state the payoff in one sentence has no
   hook yet, only a promise nobody checked.

2. **Every scroll is a prediction being confirmed.** The feed is filtered as
   noise because it matches what the brain expected. Three things break that:
   a pattern interrupt (a prediction violated), a curiosity gap (a known gap in
   what they know), and loss aversion (the cost of scrolling past, which the
   source puts at roughly twice the pull of an equivalent gain, citing the
   classic finding). A hook that opens none of the three isn't weak, it's
   invisible. Counterintuitive claims travel because a contradiction can't be
   filtered; the brain has to stop and resolve it.

3. **A hook has three parts, in a fixed order, inside seven seconds.**
   Interrupt (0-3s), gap (3-5s), stake (by 7s). Context, credentials, and
   greetings go after that line or nowhere. The specificity test: "how to grow
   on social" opens no gap because nothing precise is withheld; "the exact
   caption structure that took my posts from 2% to 11%" opens a sharp one, and
   precision reads as evidence.

4. **Keep two loops open at all times.** Retention is several promises at
   different timescales: a macro loop across the video, section loops, and
   sentence-level micro loops. Closing one is safe only while another is
   outstanding. Front-loading the core insight in the first thirty seconds
   closes the gap and removes the reason to stay. Release it in components so
   each answer raises the next question. *Check:* mark the script every five
   seconds and count open loops. Anywhere the count hits zero is where the
   retention graph already has a step.

5. **Write for a muted feed.** A large share of first views are sound-off, so
   the overlay is the hook. The first overlay lands inside the first second
   and carries the interrupt as a fragment, not a sentence: "Morning routine"
   → "killing productivity" → "Here's why". The fragments reopen the loop the
   spoken sentence just closed.

## The structure, by the clock

| Window | Job | What fails here |
|--------|-----|-----------------|
| 0-3s | Interrupt. No greeting, no setup. | A good interrupt buried behind context. |
| 3-7s | Gap, then stake. The promise crystallises. | A broad, improvable promise. |
| 8-15s | Close the four silent exits: "I already know this", "this looks low quality", "I don't trust this person", "this will be boring". Closers: novelty, production signal, evidence or results, a reason it's not a lecture. | Answering them out of order, or not at all. |
| 15-45s | The valley. Hook has worn off, content hasn't proved itself. Deliver an early win, restate the macro loop, open a section loop. | Background, credentials, "first, a bit of context". |

Dopamine is anticipation, not reward: it releases on the promise and is
sustained by a credible delay of the payoff. The moment the viewer suspects
bait, attention doesn't decline, it drops. Break the hook's contract once and
you keep the view; break it twice and you've taught the audience your promises
are unreliable, which retention doesn't recover from.

## Five open-loop shapes

| Shape | Mechanism | Example from the source |
|-------|-----------|-------------------------|
| Mystery | Outcome stated, method withheld | "Zero to a hundred thousand followers in three months, with something nobody talks about." |
| Tension | A contradiction the brain must resolve | "Everything you've been told about morning routines is working against you." |
| Incomplete story | A narrative begun and left hanging | "Someone at Google sent me a message that changed how I think about this." |
| Countdown | A promised count the viewer tracks | "Three things are quietly killing your content, and almost everyone does the third one." |
| Transformation | A before implying an after | "This is what happened after I stopped doing what every dermatologist recommends." |

Each is a debt the ending has to settle. Four older framing levers still carry
weight: contrast ("most people do X, here's what works"), identity ("if you're
the kind of person who cares about this"), availability (a recalled experience
that feels like evidence), and commitment (a rhetorical question the viewer
answers internally, which predicts staying).

## Pre-publish checklist

**First fifteen seconds**
- A pattern interrupt lands inside the first second.
- The curiosity gap is open by second five.
- The personal stake is clear by second seven.
- A text overlay carries the hook with the sound off.

**Everything after**
- Forward momentum through the 15-45s valley.
- Every section transition is bridged by a loop.
- Every loop opened is closed, explicitly, by the end.
- The content delivers the exact thing the hook promised.

## Two working rules

- **Let the graph name the edit points.** Watch-time percentage beats views;
  sharp drops are timestamps for the sentence that lost the room, not verdicts
  on the topic. Film two hooks for the same content, publish both, compare
  retention instead of guessing.
- **Build our own library.** Hook styles don't transfer cleanly between
  niches. Keep a record of the company's own high performers and read them for
  common elements rather than importing a structure that worked for someone
  else's audience.
